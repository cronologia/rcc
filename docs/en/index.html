Chronology of the Catholic Charismatic Renewal — global origins and the Brazilian movement (1967–present)

An open, source-referenced chronology of the Catholic Charismatic Renewal (Renovação Carismática Católica): the 1967 Duquesne origin, the papal receptions, the arrival and boom in Brazil (Rahm, Dougherty, Canção Nova, Shalom), the Brazilian bishops' 1994 guidelines, and the ICCRS→CHARIS institutional arc.

About

Work-in-progress dataset compiled from public sources. Movement sources (RCC Brasil, Canção Nova, CHARIS, ICCRS) are labeled as such in the references; membership numbers are the movement's own and are attributed, never asserted; the 1969-vs-1972 Brazil-arrival divergence is recorded, not resolved; sociological characterizations ('pentecostalization', competition with CEBs) are attributed to their authors. Corrections against primary sources are welcome.

What it is
A movement of 'baptism in the Holy Spirit' spirituality inside the Catholic Church — prayer groups, charisms, expressive worship — born among US university students in 1967 and today present worldwide, with Brazil among its largest national expressions.[1] [7]
Founding moment
The 'Duquesne Weekend', 17–19 February 1967: a retreat of Duquesne University students at The Ark and the Dove (Gibsonia, Pennsylvania) with reported experiences of baptism in the Spirit.[2] [3]
Arrival in Brazil
1969 per the movement's own account (Fr. Haroldo Rahm's prayer courses in Campinas), with national diffusion from 1972 via Fr. Eduardo Dougherty — an academic source (Prandi) writes 'mid-1970s'; the divergence is recorded. ?[7] [9]
Papal reception
Paul VI addressed the movement's Rome congress in 1975 ('a chance for the Church and for the world'); John Paul II received its leaders repeatedly from 1981; Francis created the single service CHARIS in 2019; Leo XIV addressed the Renewal on 30 May 2026.[12] [14] [40] [49]
Brazilian scale
The movement itself claimed ~3.8 million Brazilian participants in 1994 and tens of thousands of prayer groups today (self-reported, attributed); Pew's 2006 survey found more than half of Brazilian Catholics adopting charismatic practices.[7] [32]
International service
ICCRO/ICCRS (statutes approved 1993) served as the Rome office until 2019, when CHARIS — instituted by the Dicastery for Laity, Family and Life at Francis's express wish — replaced it; the Vatican frames CHARIS as a new service, not a fusion.[26] [40]

Chronology

Key events in chronological order. A ? flag marks dates not yet verified against a primary source.

1890s
1895 1895/1903 Lucca, Italy / Rome Elena Guerra's letters to Leo XIII — Elena Guerra (1835–1914), founder of the Oblate Sisters of the Holy Spirit, writes thirteen letters to Leo XIII between 1895 and 1903 urging a renewed devotion to the Holy Spirit; the encyclopedia record links the appeal to Leo XIII's 1897 encyclical 'Divinum illud munus', which established the novena to the Holy Spirit between Ascension and Pentecost. Movement bodies — Pentecost Today USA, the US national service committee, publishes the letters in English as a Renewal resource — present her as a forerunner of the Renewal; that lineage is the movement's own reading, recorded as such and not asserted here. A further movement claim, that Leo XIII consecrated the twentieth century to the Holy Spirit by intoning 'Veni Creator' on 1 January 1901, is deliberately NOT recorded: it is carried by movement accounts only and no primary act was located.[56] [57]
1900s
1901 1901/1906 Topeka / Los Angeles, USA Protestant precursor: modern Pentecostalism — The Topeka outpouring (1901) and the Azusa Street revival (1906) launch modern Pentecostalism — context for, not part of, the Catholic movement.[1]
1960s
1967 1967 Ann Arbor, Michigan, USA The Word of God community founded — Ralph Martin, Stephen Clark, Jim Cavnar and Gerry Rauch found the Word of God — matrix of the 'covenant communities'.[6]
1967 1967-02-17/19 Gibsonia, Pennsylvania, USA The Duquesne Weekend — About 25 students of Duquesne University's Chi Rho group, on retreat at The Ark and the Dove, report experiences of baptism in the Holy Spirit — the founding moment of the Catholic Charismatic Renewal. Some popular accounts say 17–18 February; 17–19 is the solid dating.[2] [3]
1967 1967-03-05 South Bend, Indiana, USA The movement reaches Notre Dame — Two weeks after Duquesne, nine people — among them Kevin and Dorothy Ranaghan — pray for baptism in the Spirit at the University of Notre Dame; the first 'international' gathering follows there on 7–9 April, seed of the annual Notre Dame conferences that would draw tens of thousands in the 1970s.[4] [5]
1969 1969 ? Campinas, Brazil Arrival in Brazil (movement's account) — US Jesuit Fr. Haroldo Rahm begins 'Prayer Courses and Studies on the Holy Spirit' in Campinas in the second half of 1969 — RCC Brasil cites a 15–17 August 1969 experience with about 60 young people — and Brazil's first prayer group is born. The academic literature (Prandi) dates the movement's Brazilian take-off to the mid-1970s; both datings are recorded.[7] [8] [9]
1970s
1972 1972 ? Brazil National diffusion begins — Rahm publishes 'Sereis Batizados no Espírito', presenting 'Catholic Pentecostalism'; Fr. Eduardo Dougherty SJ, ordained 1971, returns to Brazil and spreads retreats and prayer groups across the country (movement's account).[7]
1973 1973 ? Campinas, Brazil First National Congress of the RCC — About 50 leaders meet, organized by Dougherty, Rahm and Sr. Juliette Schuckenbrock; a second congress follows in Belo Horizonte in 1974 (movement's account).[7]
1974 1974-05-21/26 Malines, Belgium The first Malines Document — Cardinal Léon-Joseph Suenens convenes theologians (drafting by Kilian McDonnell) and publishes 'Malines Document 1' — the theological-pastoral orientations that gave the movement doctrinal respectability.[10] [11]
1975 1975-05-19 Rome Paul VI: 'a chance for the Church' — At the movement's third international congress in the Holy Year — with a Pentecost Mass in St Peter's on 18 May — Paul VI, addressing the participants in French on 19 May, asks: «Comment alors ce "renouveau spirituel" ne pourrait-il pas être une "chance" pour l'Église et pour le monde?» ('How then could this "spiritual renewal" not be a "chance" for the Church and for the world?'). The address was delivered in French; on vatican.va the Italian and English pages carry no body text, and the French page holds the authoritative original.[12] [13]
1978 1978-02-02 Cachoeira Paulista, Brazil Canção Nova founded — Fr. (later Msgr.) Jonas Abib founds the Canção Nova community — the future media powerhouse of the Brazilian Renewal.[15] [16]
1980s
1980 1980 Valinhos, Brazil Associação do Senhor Jesus — Fr. Eduardo Dougherty founds the Associação do Senhor Jesus: publications, music and the TV program 'Anunciamos o Senhor'. The same year Raniero Cantalamessa OFMCap, a preacher close to the Renewal, is appointed Preacher of the Papal Household by John Paul II — 1980 is confirmed by the Holy See Press Office biography ('nominato da Giovanni Paolo II Predicatore della Casa Pontificia nel 1980'), an independent corroboration of what had been a CHARIS-only dating.[17] [18] [51] [19]
1981 1981-05-07 Rome John Paul II receives the leaders' conference — The pope addresses the fourth international leaders' conference of the Renewal — the first of his repeated receptions.[14]
1982 1982-07-09 Fortaleza, Brazil Comunidade Católica Shalom founded — Moysés Louro de Azevedo Filho opens the 'evangelizing snack bar' in Fortaleza, with Cardinal Lorscheider present — birth of the Shalom community.[20] [21]
1982 1982/1983 Ann Arbor, USA / international Sword of the Spirit federation — A federation of covenant communities grows out of the Word of God (unified association 1982, international entity 1983; 82 communities, 45 Catholic, by 2017). In 1990 the Word of God disaffiliated from the Sword of the Spirit — per Wikipedia and contemporary press, following an order by Bishop Albert Ottenweller after a review of the community's governance. Contemporary press (Christianity Today, 16 Sept 1991; National Catholic Reporter, 18 April 1997) described 'heavy shepherding'/'discipleship' practices; in 1991 co-founder Ralph Martin acknowledged the Sword of the Spirit disciplines had been 'an ill-advised venture' that was 'especially harmful to women' and 'fostered elitism'. A faction led by Stephen Clark then formed a separate community keeping Sword of the Spirit affiliation. These are documented criticisms and a founder's own retrospective, labeled as such — not the site's characterization.[22] [6] [53] [54]
1990s
1990 1990 Valinhos / Recife, Brazil TV Século 21 and Obra de Maria — Dougherty's association launches the satellite channel TV Século 21 (today Rede Século 21); in Recife, Gilberto Gomes Barbosa founds the Obra de Maria community, focused on pilgrimages and missions.[23] [24] [25]
1993 1993-09-14 Rome Pontifical recognition of ICCRS — The Pontifical Council for the Laity approves the statutes of the movement's international office, renamed ICCRS (International Catholic Charismatic Renewal Services).[26] [27]
1994 1994 ? Brazil The Brazilian boom — RCC Brasil itself estimated ~3.8 million participants in 1994 — a self-reported figure, attributed to the movement.[7]
1994 1994-11-27 Brazil CNBB Document 53 — The Brazilian bishops' conference unanimously approves 'Orientações Pastorais sobre a Renovação Carismática Católica' (approved 27 November 1994 by the Permanent Council, after a study begun at the April 1994 general assembly): it recognizes the movement's fruits while imposing cautions on Bible reading, liturgy, healing practices and the relation to the local Church — the episcopate's principal 'brakes' document. No later CNBB document has superseded it; on its 20th-and-50th-anniversary reflections commentators note Doc 53 remained in force. The full text is published by Edições CNBB (the bishops' own house, digital edition); free full-text reproductions circulate via Catholic sites and a PDF mirror.[28] [52] [29] [30]
1995 1995-09-23 Gaithersburg, Maryland, USA Mother of God Community placed under review — The Mother of God Community (Gaithersburg, Maryland; traced to a 1966 prayer awakening among laywomen at Our Lady of Mercy, Potomac) is reviewed at the instruction of Cardinal James A. Hickey, Archbishop of Washington, who in 1994 ordered both a self-assessment and an outside assessment. Per the encyclopedia record the external reviewer, Sr. Elizabeth McDonough, reported that members and former members described overreaching control by community leaders, including arranged marriages and scrutiny of members' finances. On 23 September 1995 Hickey addressed the community, calling it 'a gift from the Lord' while stating that lay people within it 'had too much personal authority over other people's lives', asking for leadership changes and for personal information on members to be destroyed. These are the archdiocese's and the reviewer's findings as reported, attributed — not this site's characterization. A wave-10 mining report gave the address as 25 September and named a 'Baltimore' bishop 'Nathan Hickey'; the source consulted here gives 23 September, Washington, and James A. Hickey.[60]
1998 1998-07/09 São Paulo, Brazil Padre Marcelo Rossi's record album — 'Músicas para Louvar ao Senhor', recorded at Masses of the young priest Marcelo Rossi and released by PolyGram, sells over 3.3 million copies — the best-selling album in Brazilian phonographic history — inaugurating the era of stadium Masses and charismatic mass media.[31]
2000s
2006 2006-09-24/26 Washington DC / Rome Emmanuel Milingo excommunicated — Emmanuel Milingo, Archbishop of Lusaka from 1969 to 1983 and internationally known for a healing and exorcism ministry, was transferred to Rome in 1983 — per the encyclopedia record over what were described as inappropriate use of his office of exorcism and a role in causing divisions. He married Maria Sung in 2001 in a ceremony presided over by Sun Myung Moon of the Unification Church, drawing a public canonical admonition from Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger in July 2001. On 24 September 2006 he consecrated four married men as bishops without papal mandate; the Holy See Press Office announced the resulting latae sententiae excommunication on 26 September 2006, and he was dismissed from the clerical state on 17 December 2009. Recorded because his charismatic healing ministry made him a widely cited figure in and around the Renewal; the canonical judgments are the Holy See's, reported as such.[61]
2006 2006-10 USA (survey, 10 countries) Pew: a 'pentecostalized' Brazil — The Pew Forum's 'Spirit and Power' survey finds 'renewalists' comprising roughly half of Brazil's population, and more than half of Brazilian Catholics adopting charismatic elements (divine healing, tongues, expressive worship) — the analysts' framing of a 'pentecostalization' of Brazilian Catholicism is theirs, not the movement's.[32] [33]
2008 2008-10/11 Rome Canção Nova recognized by the Holy See — The Pontifical Council for the Laity recognizes Canção Nova as an international private association of the faithful (decree ad experimentum; definitive recognition later — the dates are distinct).[46]
2010s
2012 2012-02-22 Rome Shalom's definitive pontifical recognition — The Shalom community receives definitive pontifical recognition.[20] [21]
2014 2010s ? Brazil The RCC and Brazilian politics (analysts' account) — Social scientists (Carranza and others) describe the RCC's 'Ministério Fé e Política' backing charismatic candidates — e.g. federal deputy Diego Garcia (elected 2014, 2018) — as a response to Pentecostal political growth; the studies also record heterogeneous and internally contested engagement. The framing is the researchers', attributed.[34] [35] [36]
2014 2014-11-13 Latin America (survey) Pew: 'charismatic' Catholics across Latin America — Pew Research Center publishes 'Religion in Latin America' (fieldwork October 2013 – February 2014), reporting that a median of 40% of Catholics across Latin America describe themselves as 'charismatic'. Reported exposure to charismatic practice at Mass varies sharply: majorities in the Dominican Republic (77%), Honduras (61%) and Paraguay (60%) said they had witnessed such practices, while majorities in Uruguay (63%), Argentina (61%) and Puerto Rico (60%) said they never occur in their services. The 'charismatic' label is the survey's and is self-reported; these are a survey's figures, not the movement's, and are not comparable with movement membership counts. A wave-10 mining report attributed to this study a 58% figure for Brazil and about 20% for Argentina; neither was confirmed on the report page fetched for this entry, so neither is recorded.[58]
2017 2017-06-03 Rome (Circus Maximus) Golden Jubilee with Francis — An ecumenical Pentecost vigil with Pope Francis at the Circus Maximus gathers ~50,000 charismatics and Pentecostals from over 125 countries, with preaching by Cantalamessa and pastor Giovanni Traettino.[38] [39]
2019 2018-12-08/2019-06-09 Rome CHARIS replaces ICCRS — The Dicastery for Laity, Family and Life institutes CHARIS (Catholic Charismatic Renewal International Service) at Francis's express wish; its statutes take effect at Pentecost 2019, when ICCRS and the Catholic Fraternity cease activities. The Vatican insists it is a new service, not a fusion. Francis receives the leaders on 8 June 2019.[40] [41] [42]

2022 2022-12-12 Cachoeira Paulista, Brazil Death of Msgr. Jonas Abib — Canção Nova's founder dies at 85.[15]
2024 2024 Campinas, Brazil Death of Fr. Haroldo Rahm — The pioneer of the Brazilian Renewal — and founder of the Fazenda do Senhor Jesus addiction-recovery work — dies in Campinas.[47] [48]
2024 2024-10-20 Rome Canonisation of Elena Guerra — Elena Guerra, beatified on 26 April 1959 by John XXIII, is canonised by Francis on 20 October 2024. Movement accounts read the canonisation as recognition of a forerunner of the Renewal; the canonisation is a documented act, the lineage reading is the movement's.[56]
2026 2026-05-30 Rome Leo XIV addresses the Renewal — Leo XIV — who accompanied the Renewal during his Peruvian episcopate — receives the RCC/CHARIS, speaking on baptism in the Spirit, praise, communion and mission; he had presided at the Pentecost Mass of the Jubilee of Movements on 8 June 2025.[49] [50]

Diffusion of the Renewal — a branching timeline

How the 1967 Duquesne experience spread: to the US campuses within weeks, to Brazil at the end of the decade, and — from the Brazilian trunk — into the new communities that now define the movement's public face. Fork years mark first foundation; the Brazil arrival date is itself contested and is flagged on the figure.

Institutional arc

Two parallel institutional lines. The international service passed from ICCRO to ICCRS (statutes 1993) to CHARIS (2019) — the Vatican's own formulation is that CHARIS is a new service, not a fusion, shown here as a dashed step rather than a continuation. The covenant-communities line runs from the Word of God (Ann Arbor, 1967) into the Sword of the Spirit federation, from which the Word of God withdrew in 1990.

How big is the Renewal? — contested numbers

Two different questions, two different methods. The movement counts its own participants; Pew surveys the whole population. Both bear on 'how big is the Brazilian Renewal', but they are not the same measurement — so each series keeps its own axis, its own unit and its own source label, and they are never stacked on one scale.

These series use different units (absolute participants vs. share of a population) and different methods (movement self-report vs. external survey). They are shown on separate axes and are not directly comparable — only their sources make either number meaningful.

Elena Guerra

Italy

Italian religious of Lucca (1835–1914), founder of the Oblate Sisters of the Holy Spirit; wrote thirteen letters to Leo XIII between 1895 and 1903 urging renewed devotion to the Holy Spirit. Francis MacNutt

USA

Dominican priest (1925–2020), prominent in the Renewal's healing-prayer ministry from the late 1960s. Left the Dominican Order to marry in February 1980; received a dispensation in 1993, when the marriage was celebrated in church. Died 12 January 2020, aged 94. A wave-10 report placed his baptism in the Spirit at a 1967 Tennessee retreat; that detail is unconfirmed and is not recorded.[62]

Michael Scanlan TOR

USA

Franciscan (1931–2017); president of the College of Steubenville — later Franciscan University of Steubenville — from 1974 for 26 years, then chancellor, and a leading figure of the US charismatic covenant-community movement with roles in the Word of God and the Sword of the Spirit. Per the encyclopedia record he led the Servants of Christ the King community until he was asked to step down in 1991 amid concerns about control over members' personal lives — attributed criticism, not this site's characterization. Died 7 January 2017, aged 85.[63]

Covenant communities (international)

Word of God (1967), People of Praise (South Bend, 1971), Sword of the Spirit (1982/83), Catholic Fraternity (ceased 2019 into CHARIS); the 1990s authoritarianism controversies around the Word of God are documented criticism, labeled as such. Critics and former members have likewise described People of Praise's covenant governance and defined gender roles (a member's 'head'; the 'woman leader' role, until 2017 called 'handmaid') as authoritarian — attributed criticism that drew wide media scrutiny in 2020 around Judge Amy Coney Barrett's reported association; the community states that members retain freedom of conscience under such direction. Disambiguation & nuance

The Renewal sits at several contested boundaries; these cards keep them straight.

RCC ≠ Protestant Pentecostalism

The movement shares practices (glossolalia, healing, praise worship) but is Catholic — sacramental, Marian, in communion with Rome, with explicit papal receptions from Paul VI (1975) to Leo XIV (2026). The 'pentecostalization of Catholicism' reading is a sociologists' framing (Prandi, Pew), not a self-description. Sympathetic narrators draw the line differently, and that divergence is itself data: CBN's 50th-anniversary feature (The 700 Club, a Protestant Pentecostal-charismatic broadcaster) tells one continuous Spirit-driven story running from Elena Guerra's letters to Leo XIII through the 1901 Kansas outpouring and the Azusa Street revival to Duquesne 1967 — presenting the Pentecostal origins as part of the same family history rather than as context.[12] [9] [32] [59]

RCC vs Liberation Theology / CEBs

The reading that the Renewal 'competed' with the base communities and represented a 'conservative renewal' (the title of Prandi's study) belongs to academic commentators; the movement describes itself as complementary to the local Church. The liberation-theology side of that story lives in the sibling tl project.[9] [37]

Charismatic ≠ automatically conservative in politics

There is a 'Ministério Fé e Política' and there are RCC-linked candidates, but the case studies show heterogeneous and internally contested engagement — always attributed to the analysts.[34]

The movement ≠ the new communities

RCC Brasil (the ecclesial movement) and the 'new communities' (Canção Nova, Shalom, Obra de Maria) spring from the same charismatic current but hold distinct canonical statutes.[21] [46]

CHARIS: new service, not fusion

Press accounts spoke of a 'fusion' of ICCRS and the Catholic Fraternity; the Vatican and CHARIS insist CHARIS is a new service that ended both predecessors (9 June 2019). This site uses the Vatican's formulation.[40] [41]
